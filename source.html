01The business owner
Scenario 01 / Bank statement

The business owner who did everything right

She runs a fourteen-person practice in Fremont. $1.1 million in receipts. Her CPA structured the entity, maxed the retirement plan, took Section 179 and captured QBI.

Line 31 came out at $148,000.

She is delighted. Her lender is not.

3.5× the qualifying income. Same business, same bank account, same woman. Her CPA did not cost her the house - the form did.

For the CPAs in the room: a CPA-prepared profit-and-loss statement can often support an expense factor well below the default 50%. That letter can be worth more to your client than another deduction.  -  Figures illustrative, not an offer or commitment. Statement periods, expense factors and eligibility vary by program and lender.

02The portfolio
Scenario 02 / Asset depletion

The buyer with $3.2 million and no income

He sold his stake two years ago. Fifty-one years old. $3.2 million in a brokerage account, another $900,000 in retirement.

No W-2 since, and no intention of another one.

He wants a $2.8 million house and he is putting 40% down.

No employer. No W-2. Documented, underwritable income.

So why borrow at all? He could write the check. Because writing the check means liquidating $1.7 million - long-term gains, net investment income tax, and California on top. The mortgage is not the expensive option. It is the tax-efficient one.

You spent this morning learning exactly that. This is the same lesson with a deed attached.  -  Figures illustrative, not an offer or commitment. Variation  /  full doc + asset depletion

The W-2 earner who ran out of ratio, not money

Staff engineer. $420,000 in base and bonus, fully documented, W-2 in hand. $1.8 million in vested company stock.

He wants a $3.2 million house with 25% down.

Underwriting puts him at 57% debt-to-income and stops.
W-2 income, documented$35,000 / mo
Monthly obligations$19,900 / mo
DTI on the W-2 alone Declined57%
Asset depletion, $1.8M ÷ 120 months+ $15,000 / mo
Blended DTI Approved40%
He never sold a share, and he never pledged one. Pledging would have made it worse - a securities-backed line is one more monthly obligation on the very ratio that was already failing.

Vested shares are an asset, and agency underwriting will not turn an asset into income. Blending full documentation with asset depletion does exactly that: no liquidation event, no capital gain, no margin call.  -  Figures illustrative, not an offer or commitment. Eligible account types, divisors, concentration limits and maximum DTI vary by program and lender.

03The two houses
Scenario 03 / Bridge

The family with $1.3 million in the house they already own

Their home has done exactly what they hoped it would do. It is worth $1.9 million, with $600,000 left on it.

The family has grown, and the house that fits them now is $2.4 million.

The down payment already exists. It is sitting in the walls of the home they are living in today.
To buy, they need the equity
and the equity requires selling
↓   deadlock   ↑
To sell, they need somewhere to go
and somewhere to go requires buying
The bridge
12 months / no interest / no payments
  1. Borrow against the equity in the departing residence
  2. Make a clean, non-contingent offer on the new home
  3. Close, and move
  4. Sell on your timeline, not a buyer's
  5. Retire the bridge out of the sale proceeds
Why it clears underwriting
No monthly payment means no monthly obligation, so the bridge never enters the debt-to-income calculation at all. And if they rent the departing house rather than rushing the sale, that rental income counts toward qualifying too.
They never had a money problem. They had a sequencing problem.

A contingent offer in this market is usually a losing offer.  -  Figures illustrative, not an offer or commitment. The 12-month, no-interest, no-payment structure is a specific program with its own eligibility, equity and exit requirements, and is subject to change and to underwriting approval. Departing-residence rental income has its own documentation and vacancy-factor requirements.

Neither of them is short of money. Both of them are early. Underwriting cannot measure income that has not arrived yet, so this program stops trying and measures what is already there: the reserves, the credit, and the down payment.
